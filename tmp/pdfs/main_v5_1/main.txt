    A structure-preserving discontinuous Galerkin method for Maxwell’s equations
                   based on a discrete Poisson bracket formulation

                    Martí Circuns-Duxansa , Eloi Ruiz-Gironésa , Josep de la Puentea , Xevi Rocaa,∗
        a Computer Applications in Science and Engineering, Barcelona Supercomputing Center, E-08034, Barcelona, Spain




Abstract
We develop a structure-preserving discontinuous Galerkin method for the time-domain Maxwell’s equations based
on a discrete Poisson bracket formulation. Starting from the Hamiltonian structure of the electric-magnetic field
system, we construct a discontinuous finite element discretization of the underlying Poisson bracket that preserves
skew-symmetry and yields a finite-dimensional Hamiltonian system of ordinary differential equations for the discrete
field coefficients. To obtain a fully discrete scheme suitable for long-time wave propagation, we couple this semi-
discretization with explicit symplectic partitioned Runge-Kutta time integration. The resulting method preserves
the relevant discrete geometric structure and exhibits the bounded, non-secular energy behaviour characteristic of
symplectic integration. Numerical experiments—including plane-wave propagation and cavity-mode benchmarks—
demonstrate high-order convergence, long-time physical fidelity, and robust conservation properties.
Keywords: Maxwell’s equations, discontinuous Galerkin method, structure-preserving discretization, Hamiltonian
systems, Poisson bracket


1. Introduction

    Time-domain simulation of electromagnetic waves is central to applications ranging from antenna design and
scattering to photonics and plasma modeling. At the PDE level, Maxwell’s equations encode not only wave propaga-
tion and source coupling, but also tightly constrained geometric and variational structure: curl operators connected
by Stokes’ theorem, divergence constraints, and conservation laws such as Poynting’s theorem. In lossless media
and in closed domains, these features yield conserved quantities -most prominently electromagnetic energy- that are
physically meaningful and numerically diagnostic over long time horizons.
    A persistent difficulty in computational electromagnetics is that high-order spatial accuracy alone does not guar-
antee physically faithful long-time behavior. Even stable schemes may exhibit slow drift in invariants, accumulated
phase error, or spurious modes, especially when simulations require many wave periods or when multiscale resolutions
enforce stringent CFL conditions. This motivates structure-preserving discretizations: algorithms designed so that
discrete solutions inherit key geometric identities and invariants of the continuous system. In geometric numerical
integration, symplectic methods are the flagship example for Hamiltonian ODEs; they are designed to preserve the
symplectic structure and thereby yield near-conservation of invariants over long integrations [32].
    Classical electromagnetic solvers set the baseline. The finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) scheme introduced
by Yee remains foundational in time-domain electromagnetics and is supported by extensive practical developments
[17, 23, 12]. While FDTD is efficient on structured grids, unstructured geometries and high-order accuracy often

    Discontinuous Galerkin time-domain (DGTD) methods further offer element-local mass matrices, high-order
approximations, and flexibility for heterogeneous media and local refinement; they have a mature analysis literature
addressing stability, convergence, and energy behavior under specific flux and time choices [7, 8, 16, 18, 22, 10].
    A particularly direct route to conservation is the bracket-first philosophy: discretize the Poisson bracket and
Hamiltonian functional so that discrete skew-symmetry is built in, and conservation follows from algebra rather than
from delicate cancellations in the PDE form. In fluid dynamics and geophysical flows, this idea is classic [3](. In
finite element settings, [34] demonstrated that discretizing a bilinear Poisson bracket with discontinuous elements can
produce a semi-discrete Hamiltonian ODE system with mass/energy conservation properties, providing a template
for hyperbolic systems that include Maxwell-type equations. In electromagnetic plasma simulations, discrete Poisson
bracket constructions also underpin modern structure-preserving Vlasov-Maxwell solvers, where bracket properties
and a compatible field space are central design constraints [19].
    The closest modern comparison class is the emerging family of symplectic-Hamiltonian finite element methods,
which explicitly constructs space discretizations yielding a discrete Hamiltonian ODE and couples them with sym-
plectic time integrators to obtain non-drifting approximations of key physical quantities [28, 29, 30, 31, 26]. Our
focus differs in that we organize the discretization around an explicit discrete Poisson bracket defined in DG spaces,
and we emphasize explicit symplectic time-marching and DGTD-style benchmarking to assess accuracy, stability,
and long-time fidelity.
    In contrast to the recent family of Symplectic Finite Element Methods, the scheme presented in this paper applies
directly to a discrete analog of the Poisson bracket in space, naturally yielding a Hamiltonian system of ODEs. In
time, we combine the resulting semi-discrete scheme with suitable symplectic time-marching schemes for long-time
simulations.
    This work makes three contributions. First, starting from the Hamiltonian formulation of Maxwell’s equations in
the E − H variables, we construct a discrete Poisson bracket posed on discontinuous finite element spaces. The re-
sulting semi-discrete evolution is therefore obtained directly from a finite-dimensional Hamiltonian structure, rather
than from a DG weak form postulated a priori and analyzed afterward. Second, we show that the semi-discrete equa-
tions induced by this bracket coincide with a discontinuous Galerkin formulation with well-defined face contributions
arising from broken integration by parts. In this sense, the numerical flux structure is not an external design choice,
but emerges from the discrete Hamiltonian framework. Third, we combine the bracket-based DG semi-discretization
with explicit symplectic partitioned Runge–Kutta time integrators, thereby obtaining a fully discrete method tailored
to long-time wave propagation. This construction yields a high-order scheme that is locally discontinuous in space
and designed to exhibit non-drifting behavior of physically relevant invariants over long simulation times.
    The main contribution of this work is the systematic derivation of a discontinuous Galerkin discretization of
Maxwell’s equations from a discrete Poisson bracket. In this construction, the variational formulation and the nu-
merical traces arise directly from the underlying Hamiltonian structure. The resulting semi-discrete scheme preserves
the weak electric and magnetic Gauss constraints and, in the source-free case under compatible boundary conditions,
exactly conserves the discrete electromagnetic energy. The fully discrete formulation is obtained by means of a sym-
plectic partitioned Runge–Kutta method, which preserves the discrete symplectic structure and provides a favorable
long-time behavior of the electromagnetic energy.
    In the present work, the term structure-preserving refers primarily to retaining, at the semi-discrete level, the
Hamiltonian generation mechanism and the skew-symmetry of the underlying bracket. These properties are sufficient
to produce a finite-dimensional Hamiltonian evolution and to recover the DG weak form from the discrete bracket
construction. We do not claim here that the discrete bracket satisfies a Jacobi identity in full generality. From
the viewpoint of Poisson geometry, the discrete structure retained in this paper is the skew-symmetric Hamiltonian
coupling induced by the bracket, the algebraic feature most directly tied to the conservation properties studied below.
    The results presented in this work are threefold. At the structural level, we establish the algebraic properties of the

differential form as follows [33]:

                                                ∇ · (εE) = ρ,                                                       (1c)

in Ω × (0, T ], equipped with the following boundary condition

                                              n × E = gE on Γ × (0, T ],
   and initial conditions

                                                 E(x, 0) = E0 , on Ω,
                                                 H(x, 0) = H0 , on Ω.

    Here, E and H are the electric and magnetic fields; ε and µ are the permittivity and permeability; and ρ and
J represent the scalar charge density function and the vector current density function, respectively. Equations (1a)
to (1d) assume a homogeneous, non-dispersive medium where ε, µ, ρ, J, and gE are independent of time. It also
disregards polarization and magnetization effects, implying a linear relationship between the fields and charges.
We also assume that the current J is solenoidal, J = ∇ × J× . Equations (1a) to (1d) account for full time-
dependent behavior without approximating steady-state conditions. It also disregards polarization and magnetization
effects, implying a linear relationship between the fields and charges. The set of Equations (1a) to (1d) forms the
basis of classical electromagnetic phenomena and has been extensively studied in CEM, particularly in time-domain
formulations.

physical laws; however, some numerical schemes often fail to preserve these conserved quantities, potentially leading
to discrepancies in complex electromagnetic scenarios.

    In this section, we state a semi-discrete method by discretizing the Hamiltonian structure identified in Section 2.2;

semi-discrete weak form is then equivalent to a discontinuous Galerkin formulation, with face contributions arising
naturally from the broken bracket structure.

6. Concluding remarks

    In this work, we have developed a structure-preserving discontinuous Galerkin method for the time-domain
Maxwell’s equations based on a discrete Poisson bracket formulation. Exploiting the Hamiltonian structure of
Maxwell’s equations, we constructed a semi-discrete approximation via a consistent discrete Poisson bracket, resulting
in a finite-dimensional Hamiltonian system of ordinary differential equations.
    The performance of the proposed method was assessed through a convergence study. Moreover, the scheme
exhibits non-drifting behavior of key physical invariants, including energy, magnetic charge, linear momentum, and
angular momentum, thereby confirming its structure-preserving nature and long-time stability.

Appendix E. Lemma 2

   Lemma 2. Let n ∈ R3 be fixed and let a and b be vector-valued functions. Then,

                                              ⟨n × a, b⟩ = − ⟨a, n × b⟩ .                                         (E.1)

Proof. Using the cyclic property of the inner product,

                                                ⟨n × a, b⟩ = ⟨n, a × b⟩ .

Since a × b = −b × a,
                                              ⟨n, a × b⟩ = − ⟨n, b × a⟩ .
Applying the cyclic property again,
                                             − ⟨n, b × a⟩ = − ⟨a, n × b⟩ .
Therefore
                                              ⟨n × a, b⟩ = − ⟨a, n × b⟩ .
